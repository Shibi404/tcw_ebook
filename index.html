Preface

Printing on a university campus still works the way it did twenty years ago. There is a counter, it keeps fixed hours, it takes cash, and a queue forms in front of it an hour before every submission deadline. Almost every other campus transaction has moved to self-service in that time. Printing has not.

PrintPod is a self-service printing kiosk designed for universities. A student uploads a document, chooses how they want it printed, pays from their phone or campus card, and collects the printout. No counter, no staff, no cash. This ebook describes what the system does, how it is built, and what it takes to run one.

It is written for two readers. Administrators and facility managers evaluating a self-service printing system will find the case for it in Part 1, the technical and operational detail in Parts 2 and 4, and the costs in Part 5. Students and staff who will use a kiosk need only Chapter 7, a step-by-step guide to printing a document, and the questions answered in Chapter 12.

The ebook covers what PrintPod is, how it is built, how it is used by students and administrators, how it is deployed and maintained, and what it costs to operate. It does not cover procurement or tendering processes, vendor selection, or any institution's specific IT and data policies. Those vary too widely between institutions to be treated usefully here.

Read it in order if you are deciding whether to deploy a kiosk. If you already know what PrintPod is and want one answer, use the table of contents. The chapters are self-contained, and where detail is not repeated, a cross-reference points to the chapter that holds it.

The case

Chapter

The campus printing problem

[Chapter opening: 2–3 sentences framing printing as a recurring, time-critical need on campus, and stating what this chapter will establish — that the current arrangement fails students and costs the institution.]

A day in the life of a student deadline

It is 9 p.m., and a second-year student has a report due at 9 a.m. tomorrow. Her instructor wants three printed copies. The library counter closed at six, and the only print shop outside the gate has a queue of students with the same deadline. She stands in line for forty minutes, pays in cash, and waits while the shopkeeper looks for change. Then her file is opened on a shared computer that other people are also using.

Where the current system breaks down

  • Limited operating hours. Library and department counters close in the evening, but deadlines and late-night study do not.
  • Queues at peak times. Printing demand rises sharply before submission dates, and one counter with one operator cannot absorb it.
  • Cash and change problems. Small payments in cash need exact change, and both students and shopkeepers lose time to this.
  • Staff time on a non-academic task. Library and office staff spend part of every day on printing instead of on the work they were hired to do.
  • Privacy. Documents left in an output tray can be taken or read by anyone who passes. At a shared counter this applies to assignments, project reports and sometimes personal documents.
  • No record. Counter printing leaves no reliable log of what was printed, by whom, or what was paid, so disputes and errors are hard to settle.

What it costs the institution

Administrators rarely see printing as a cost centre, but it uses resources across the institution. Part of this cost is human: staff time diverted from teaching support and library services. Part is physical: counter space and equipment that need upkeep. The rest is organisational: end-of-day cash reconciliation and a steady flow of small complaints about queues, wrong prints and missing change. Because none of this is tracked, it is easy to underestimate. Costs are discussed qualitatively here, and figures appear in Chapter 14.

The main cost areas are:

  • Staff hours spent operating counters and handling print requests
  • Counter space and equipment taken up by manual printing
  • Daily cash handling and reconciliation
  • Complaints about waiting times, errors and payment disputes
  • Paper and toner wasted on reprints and uncollected jobs

What a solution must do

A printing solution for a university should meet these criteria:

  1. R1: Always available. It works whenever the campus is open, not only during staff hours.
  2. R2: No cash handling. Payment is digital, with no change and no end-of-day counting.
  3. R3: Private by design. A document is never visible to anyone other than its owner.
  4. R4: Usable without training. A first-time user can print without asking for help.
  5. R5: Accountable. Every job and payment is recorded and can be traced.
  6. R6: Low staff effort. It runs without a dedicated operator and reports its own problems.

Chapter

Introducing PrintPod

[Chapter opening: 2–3 sentences introducing PrintPod as the proposed answer to the requirements just listed, without yet going into architecture.]

What it is

PrintPod is a self-service printing kiosk for universities. A student uploads a PDF, chooses print options, pays digitally and collects the printout at the kiosk, with no staff member involved. Documents are released only to the person who submitted them, and every job and payment is recorded. Administrators can monitor paper, ink and printer status remotely, so the kiosk keeps running without a dedicated operator.

Key capabilities at a glance

Capability What it means
Upload PDFs Send your file to the kiosk from your own phone or laptop
Choose print options Pick copies, colour or black and white, and single or double sides before you pay
Digital payment Pay on screen, with no cash and no change needed
Self-service collection Pick up your printout at the kiosk at any hour the campus is open
Private release Your document prints only when you are standing at the kiosk
Job queue Jobs are handled in order, so a busy period does not cause a breakdown
Printer monitoring The kiosk tracks its own paper and ink levels and reports faults
Records and reports Every print and payment is logged for you and for the administrator

How it answers each requirement

  • R1, Always available: The kiosk runs without staff, so printing is possible whenever the campus is open.
  • R2, No cash handling: Payment is digital, so there is no change to give and no cash to reconcile.
  • R3, Private by design: Files are held until the owner is at the kiosk, so no one else sees the printout.
  • R4, Usable without training: The flow is upload, choose, pay, collect, shown step by step on screen.
  • R5, Accountable: Each job and payment is logged against the user.
  • R6, Low staff effort: Remote monitoring of paper, ink and printer status replaces routine checks by staff.

Who it serves

The student. Students can print at any hour, pay without cash, and collect their documents without waiting behind a queue. Their files stay private from upload to collection.

The faculty member. Faculty can print handouts, question papers and notes without going through a department office. A record of each job is kept, so repeat prints and costs are easy to track.

The administrator. Administrators get a printing service that does not depend on staff time or cash handling. They can see usage, payments and printer status from one place and plan refills before the kiosk runs out.

Chapter

How it works

[Chapter opening: 2 sentences promising a plain-language walkthrough before the technical detail of part 2.]

The four-step cycle

Every print job on PrintPod goes through the same four steps: upload, choose, pay and collect. The order is fixed. A job cannot move on until the step before it is done, so a user is never asked to decide something before the kiosk has what it needs to show them the result. This is how the system meets requirement R4, usable without training (Section 1.4): a first-time user only ever has one thing to do next.

Step What the user does What is settled by the end of it
1 Upload Sends a file to the kiosk, either by scanning the QR code on its screen or, for a file already uploaded from the website, by showing the collection code to the scanner window or typing its six digits. The file is in the system and the kiosk can show a preview of it.
2 Choose Checks the preview, then picks copies, page range, sides, colour and paper size. What will be printed, and exactly what it will cost.
3 Pay Pays on the kiosk screen by UPI or campus card. The payment is confirmed. Printing cannot start before this.
4 Collect Takes the printout from the output tray. The job is complete and the document is deleted.

Two rules sit behind this order. The user sees the final price before paying, because Choose comes before Pay. And nothing is printed until the payment gateway has confirmed the payment, because Pay comes before Collect. The second rule matters most. It appears again in Section 3.3 and in Section 4.4.

Chapter 7 shows each step screen by screen. It splits Choose into two screens, one for the preview and one for the print settings, so it lists five steps where this chapter lists four. The process is the same. The user guide counts screens, and this chapter counts decisions.

The user journey

Take the student from Section 1.1, who needs three printed copies of a report before 9 a.m. With PrintPod her evening goes differently. She uploads the report from her hostel room on the PrintPod website and is given a collection code. She walks to the nearest kiosk, holds the code up to the scanner window, and the report appears on screen as a preview. She checks that her tables have not moved, raises the copies to three, and watches the price change as she goes. She pays by UPI, collects three copies from the tray and leaves. By then the kiosk has already deleted her file.

The figure shows the main path across the middle and the ways out of it underneath. There are two ways to start. A user who is already at the kiosk scans the QR code on its screen with a phone and picks a file. A user who prefers to upload in advance does so from the website and brings the six-digit code (Section 6.2). Either way the file reaches the system through the web app, so every screen after that looks the same.

A user can leave at several points without losing anything. A file that is too large, too long or password-protected is rejected at upload and the user is not charged (Section 6.1). Tapping Cancel Job on the preview screen returns the kiosk to its start screen with nothing charged. A job that is uploaded but never paid for expires and is deleted, four hours after a website upload or ten minutes after it has been opened at a kiosk (Section 6.4). Once a user has paid, the responsibility passes to the system. If the job then cannot be printed, the user is refunded under the rules in Chapter 13.

What happens behind the scenes

The user sees four steps. Behind them, three parts of the system take turns: the kiosk, the cloud backend and the payment gateway. The backend is involved in every step, because the kiosk never accepts a file or confirms a payment on its own. Chapter 4 describes each part. The table shows what happens behind the screen at each step.

Step What happens behind the screen
Upload The user's phone opens the PrintPod web app, which sends the file to the backend. The backend checks it against the limits in Section 6.1 and converts anything that is not a PDF. It creates a job record. For a kiosk upload it links the job to that kiosk's session through the QR code, which is valid for five minutes and for that one session only. For a website upload it issues a collection code instead, as a QR code to scan and as six digits to type. It then sends a preview to the kiosk.
Choose The kiosk sends each setting to the backend, which applies the current rates (Section 13.2) and returns the price. The price is fixed when the user reaches the payment screen, so the amount paid is the amount shown.
Pay The backend asks the payment gateway for a payment request for that amount and that job. The kiosk shows it as a QR code or waits for a campus-card tap. When the money has been taken, the gateway confirms to the backend, and only then does the backend mark the job as paid.
Collect The backend releases the paid job to the kiosk, which puts it in its queue and prints it. When the kiosk reports that printing is done, the backend writes the transaction record and deletes the document, both its own copy and the copy spooled to the kiosk, within sixty seconds (Section 6.5).

Note

PrintPod does not rely on what a user's phone shows. The backend waits for the gateway's own confirmation, so a job never prints on an unconfirmed payment.

While all this happens, the kiosk also reports to the backend, whether or not anyone is using it. It sends its state, its paper and toner levels and any fault. These reports feed the dashboard and the alerts described in Chapter 8. If the backend hears nothing from a kiosk for five minutes, it marks the kiosk Offline, and the kiosk takes no new jobs until the connection returns (Section 8.3).

The same structure makes failures predictable. The backend holds the job and the gateway holds the payment, so the system always knows which of the two succeeded, and Section 4.6 lists what happens in each case. Every step also adds a line to the job's timeline, which is what an administrator reads when a student reports a problem (Section 8.7).

System architecture

[Chapter opening: 2 sentences naming the four components the chapter will describe and the relationship between them.]

Overview

PrintPod has four parts: the kiosk, the cloud backend, the payment gateway and the admin portal. Users reach the system through a fifth, lighter component, the PrintPod web app, which runs in the browser on their own phone or laptop and needs no installation. Figure 4.1 shows how the parts connect.

The backend sits in the middle. Every other part talks to the backend and not to the others. A file travels from the user's phone to the backend and then to the kiosk. A payment is confirmed by the gateway to the backend, and then the backend tells the kiosk to print. An administrator's action in the portal goes to the backend, which applies it. This keeps the kiosk simple, and it gives the system one place where every job and payment is recorded (requirement R5).

Part Where it runs What it does Who uses it
Kiosk On campus, at the site Shows the screens, takes the payment, queues jobs and prints Students and faculty
Cloud backend Hosted online, outside the campus Receives and converts files, prices jobs, runs the queue, keeps the records, deletes documents The other parts only
Payment gateway Run by a third-party provider Processes payments and refunds and confirms the result The backend only
Admin portal A web page served by the backend Shows status, prices and records, and handles refunds and reports Operators and administrators
Web app In the user's own browser Uploads files and shows prices, kiosk status and recent jobs Students and faculty

Three design decisions shape the architecture.

  • One way in. Every file goes through the backend. The kiosk has no USB port and no inbox (Section 6.2), so there is no way to put a file into the unit that has not been checked and logged first.
  • A thin kiosk. The kiosk holds no accounts and no history. It keeps a document only for the job it is printing and deletes it afterwards (Section 6.5). Nothing about past users is stored on the unit.
  • Payment handled outside. The gateway handles all money. PrintPod itself holds no bank or card credentials, and no card details reach the kiosk (Section 10.3).

The kiosk

The kiosk is the only part of PrintPod that users touch. Its enclosure, printer and screen are specified in Chapter 5. This section covers the software and electronics inside it, which do five jobs.

  • Interface. The touchscreen shows the upload, preview, settings, payment and collection screens in English, Hindi or Malayalam, with the accessibility options in Section 6.6. It holds no user accounts, so nothing a user chooses is kept once the session ends.
  • Print spooler. The spooler receives a paid job from the backend, holds it in a queue and sends it to the printer one job at a time (Section 6.4). It is the only place on the kiosk where a document exists, and it is emptied when the job is done.
  • Payment hardware. The screen shows the dynamic QR code for UPI payments, and the NFC reader takes a campus-card tap. The kiosk does not store or process payment credentials. It passes the request on and receives only the result.
  • Status reporting. The kiosk reports its state, its paper and toner levels and any printer or reader fault to the backend. These reports drive the five states in Section 8.3 and the status light along the top of the unit.
  • Connectivity. The kiosk connects over Wi-Fi 6 or Gigabit Ethernet, with 4G LTE as a fallback (Section 5.2). It always starts the connection to the backend itself, so the campus network does not need to accept incoming connections from outside.

If the kiosk loses contact with the backend, it stops taking new jobs and shows an unavailable message, because without the backend it cannot confirm a payment. Status reports are held on the kiosk and sent when the connection returns, so no records are lost.

The cloud backend

The backend is software running on servers outside the campus. It does the work that must be the same for every kiosk and every user. Apart from the kiosk that prints a job, it is the only part that ever holds a document. Its responsibilities are:

  • Receiving and converting. It accepts files from the web app and checks type, size, length and password protection (Section 6.1). It converts Word, PowerPoint, text and image files to PDF and builds the preview the kiosk shows, so the user sees the converted file before paying.
  • Pairing jobs with kiosks. It issues the QR session code, which is valid for five minutes and one session, and the collection code for a website upload, which it returns both as a QR code and as six digits. Any of them connects a job to the kiosk where the user is standing.
  • Pricing. It applies the rate table and any concession, free quota or departmental billing set by the administrator (Sections 13.2 and 13.3), and returns the price each time a setting changes.
  • Queues and timers. It holds each kiosk's queue and runs the expiry timers: four hours for a website upload that has not yet been opened at a kiosk, ten minutes for an unpaid job once it has been opened, and ten minutes again for a paid job that cannot be printed (Section 6.4).
  • Payments. It creates payment requests with the gateway, receives the confirmations and starts refunds (Section 4.4).
  • Records. It writes every job, payment, refund and administrative action to the records, and answers the admin portal.
  • Deletion. It deletes each document within sixty seconds of the job completing (Section 6.5).

The backend keeps three kinds of data, and it keeps them apart.

Data What it contains How long it is kept
Documents The uploaded file and its preview Deleted within sixty seconds of printing, four hours after a website upload that is never opened at a kiosk, ten minutes after a job is opened and not paid for, or ten minutes after payment if not printed (Section 6.5)
Transaction records Job ID, kiosk, page count, settings, price, payment reference, status and times. Never the document, its file name or its contents As set out in Section 10.6
Audit log Prices changed, refunds issued and accounts changed, each with the person's name and the time As set out in Section 10.6

The separation is deliberate. Because documents and records are stored apart, deleting a document never touches the billing record, and the records can be shown to administrators without any risk of exposing what was printed. Chapter 10 sets out how documents and records are protected.

The payment gateway

The payment gateway is a third-party service, contracted by the institution (the merchant account in Section 9.2), that handles all money in PrintPod. It supports UPI and campus cards (Section 13.1). PrintPod gives it the amount for a job and takes back only the result.

How a payment is confirmed. The backend creates a payment request with the gateway for the exact amount, tied to one job. The kiosk shows it as a dynamic QR code, or waits for a campus-card tap. When the user approves, the gateway sends its confirmation to the backend. The backend marks the job as paid on that message and no other, and only then releases the job to print. PrintPod does not act on what a user's phone shows, because only the gateway can say that the money has moved.

Why the gateway handles it all. The kiosk and the backend hold no bank or card credentials, so there are none to lose if either is compromised (Section 10.3). Each payment is also tied to one job, which is how a second payment for the same job is recognised and refunded.

How refunds work. A refund is a request from the backend to the gateway, and it goes back to the original payment method. The backend sends one automatically when a paid job fails: the full amount if no page was delivered, the amount for the unprinted pages if the job stopped part-way, and the second payment if a job was paid for twice (Section 8.7). A campus-card refund is credited within minutes. A UPI refund is sent back to the payer's bank straight away, and how soon it appears depends on the bank.

When the connection is lost. If the kiosk cannot reach the backend, it cannot get a confirmation, so it does not ask for payment at all and shows an unavailable message (Section 8.3). A user is never charged for a job that cannot start.

Settlement. The gateway pays the institution on its own settlement schedule, after fees. The portal's revenue figures are the payments the gateway has confirmed, which have not necessarily reached the bank yet (Section 13.5).

The admin portal

The admin portal is the web page through which the institution runs the service. It is served by the backend, so there is nothing to install. Operators and administrators open it in a browser and sign in with a password and a one-time code sent to their email or phone, and the session ends after 15 minutes without activity (Section 8.1). Chapter 8 explains how to use it. This section covers where it fits.

  • What it shows. Live kiosk status, jobs, payments, refunds, paper and toner levels and open alerts (Section 8.2).
  • What it can change. Prices, refunds, user accounts and kiosk registration. Each of these is limited by role. An operator can watch and acknowledge, and only an administrator can change prices, issue refunds or manage accounts (Section 8.1).
  • What it records. Every change is written to the audit log with the name of the person who made it and the time.

No role can open a document. The portal is connected to the transaction records and the status reports, and not to the document store, so the restriction does not depend on a setting that someone could change. This is requirement R3 applied to the people who run the service, and Section 10.5 describes access control in full.

Data flow for a single print job

No. Between What happens
1 Phone and backend The user scans the QR code and the web app opens, already linked to the kiosk. They pick a file, which is uploaded over an encrypted connection.
2 Backend The backend checks the file, converts it to PDF if needed and creates the job record.
3 Backend and kiosk The backend sends the preview, and the kiosk shows it.

A job uploaded from the website follows the same path. When a step fails

Each of these events appears on the job's timeline in the portal, so an administrator can see what happened without needing to see the document.

What goes wrong What the system does
The user uploads a file but never pays The job expires and the document is deleted: four hours after a website upload that is never opened at a kiosk, ten minutes after it has been opened (Section 6.4).

Hardware specifications

The kiosk unit

The PrintPod unit is a free-standing enclosure designed to stand against a wall in a corridor, foyer or library entrance. It is 1,450 mm tall, which places the touchscreen at a comfortable height for a standing adult and keeps the output tray above knee level. The front face carries, from top to bottom, the touchscreen, the QR scanner window, the card reader and the output tray. The printer sits behind a lockable panel on the left side, so paper and toner can be replaced without opening the main enclosure. A status light runs along the top edge and is visible from the far end of a corridor, which lets a passing student see that the kiosk is free before walking to it.

Consumables

The unit takes two consumables, and both can be replaced by a campus operator without a service visit.

Paper. The tray holds 500 sheets of 80 gsm A4. A second tray for A3 is fitted to units that offer A3 printing. At the usage assumed in Chapter 14, and with double-sided printing as the default, a single kiosk empties its A4 tray roughly every two days during term and far less often outside it.

Toner. The cartridge is rated at 6,000 pages at five per cent coverage, which works out to about twelve days of term-time printing on one unit. The admin portal raises a low-toner alert at fifteen per cent remaining, giving an operator nearly two days of warning rather than a surprise on a deadline morning.

Both consumables are ordinary office stock. Nothing in the kiosk is proprietary, and an institution can buy paper and cartridges from its existing supplier.

Site requirements

A location must meet five requirements before a unit can be installed.

Floor space. The unit occupies 600 mm by 550 mm. Allow 900 mm of clear space in front for a user to stand, and 400 mm of clearance on the left for the printer panel to swing open.

Power. One earthed 230 V socket within two metres, on a circuit that is not switched off with the building lighting. The unit draws 450 W while printing and 12 W on standby.

Network. Wi-Fi coverage at the kiosk position, or a wired point within three metres. Signal strength should be tested at the exact position rather than assumed from coverage elsewhere in the building.

Lighting. Enough ambient light for a user to read the screen and check a printout, and no window directly behind the unit, which makes the touchscreen hard to read for part of the day.

Mounting. The unit is bolted to the floor through four points in the base. Floor fixing is required in any location that is unsupervised outside staff hours.

Chapter

Software features

[Chapter opening: 2 sentences on what the software does for the user and for the operator.]

Supported file formats

PrintPod accepts PDF, Word documents in .docx and .doc, PowerPoint files in .pptx, plain text, and the JPG, PNG and HEIC image formats.

PDF is the recommended format and prints exactly as it appears on the user's own screen. Other formats are converted to PDF on the backend before printing, and conversion can shift the layout of a document that uses an unusual font or a complex table. The preview screen shows the converted file, not the original, so a user who checks the preview will see any shift before paying.

The maximum file size is 50 MB and the maximum length is 200 pages in a single job. A file that exceeds either limit is rejected at upload with a message naming the limit it broke, and the user is not charged. Password-protected files cannot be opened and are rejected in the same way.

Upload methods

There are two ways to get a document onto a kiosk, and both go through the same place: the PrintPod web app, at the address printed on every unit. There is nothing to install and no account to create.

Why both routes lead to the same place. Sending every upload through one web app keeps the path identical whichever way a user starts, so the preview, settings and payment screens behave the same way in both cases. It also means the kiosk itself never accepts a file directly. There is no USB port and no inbox, so there is no way to put a file into the unit without it passing through the backend first.

At the kiosk, by QR code. The user taps Upload on the kiosk and scans the QR code shown on screen with their phone camera. The code opens the PrintPod web app in the phone's browser, already linked to that kiosk, and the user picks a file from their phone storage or from a cloud drive they are signed in to. The file appears on the kiosk screen for preview within a few seconds. The QR code is valid for five minutes and belongs to that one session, so nobody else can send a file to the kiosk while it is in use.

From the website, before arriving. The user opens the PrintPod web address in any browser and uploads a file. The web app returns a collection code in two forms: a QR code to hold up to the scanner window on the front of any kiosk, and the same code as six digits to type in instead. Scanning is quicker and avoids mistyping, and the six digits stay available for anyone who would rather not hold up a phone. Either one brings the job up for preview, and the code works for that one job and stops working as soon as the job is collected or expires. This is the faster route at busy times, because the upload is finished before the user reaches the queue.

What else the web app does. Besides uploading, it shows the current price list, the live status of every kiosk on campus so a user can walk to one that is free, and the user's own recent jobs with the amount paid and the refund status where a job failed. It never shows the contents of a document, and it holds no file once the job is complete, as set out in Section 6.5.

Print settings available to the user

The settings screen offers seven options. Each one defaults to the cheapest sensible choice, so a user who changes nothing gets the lowest price for that document.

Copies defaults to one. Page range defaults to all pages. Sided defaults to double-sided, which halves the paper used. Colour defaults to monochrome. Paper size defaults to A4. Orientation defaults to the orientation of the uploaded file. Pages per sheet defaults to one.

The price shown at the foot of the screen updates as each setting changes, so a user can see the cost of switching to colour or to single-sided before committing to it.

Queue management

Each kiosk prints one job at a time and holds the rest in a queue. A user who arrives while another job is printing can still upload, preview, configure and pay, and the screen shows their position in the queue and an estimated wait in seconds. In practice a queue rarely exceeds two jobs, because a typical job finishes in well under a minute.

A job uploaded from the website is held for four hours, so that a user can send a file from a hostel room or between lectures and still collect it on the way past. Once a job has been opened at a kiosk it is held for ten minutes, and a job uploaded at the kiosk itself is on that ten-minute timer from the start; in either case it then expires and is deleted. A paid job that fails to print, because the kiosk runs out of paper for instance, is held for ten minutes so that it can be released once the kiosk is refilled. If it cannot be printed within that window, the job is deleted and the payment is refunded automatically under the rules in Chapter 13.

Document retention and deletion

A document is deleted within sixty seconds of its print job completing. Deletion covers both the copy held in the backend and the copy spooled to the kiosk, and it happens whether or not the user collects the printout.

An uploaded job that is never paid for is deleted when it expires: four hours after a website upload that is never opened at a kiosk, and ten minutes after a job has been opened. A paid job that cannot be printed is deleted after ten minutes, together with an automatic refund.

Nothing is retained beyond these windows. The transaction record kept for billing holds the page count, the price and the timestamp, and does not hold the document, its file name or its contents. Chapter 10 sets out how files are protected while they are held.

Accessibility features

The kiosk is designed to be usable by a seated wheelchair user. The touchscreen is mounted at 1,050 mm and tilted fifteen degrees upward, the output tray sits at 800 mm, and every control the user needs falls within a 1,200 mm reach from the front of the unit.

On screen, the interface offers three text sizes and a high-contrast mode, both reachable from an accessibility button present on every screen. The interface is available in English, Hindi and Malayalam, selected at the start of a session and reset when the session ends. Audio prompts can be enabled to read each screen aloud through a 3.5 mm headphone socket below the card reader, so a visually impaired user can work through the flow without a sighted assistant. A collection code can always be typed as six digits instead of being scanned, so a user who cannot aim a phone at the scanner window is not shut out of the website route.

No setting persists between sessions, because the kiosk holds no user accounts. Each user chooses what they need at the start of their own session.

Consumable alerts and refilling

Each kiosk reports its paper and toner levels to the backend continuously, and two alerts follow from those readings.

Alerts go by email, and onto the dashboard, to every operator assigned to that kiosk. Administrators get a copy of every empty alert, and of any low alert still unacknowledged after four hours. If a site is slow to refill, an administrator can raise the thresholds under Kiosks, then the kiosk's name, then Alert settings, to get an earlier warning.

Security and privacy

PrintPod focuses on protecting user documents, payment information and the physical kiosk.

Document security

User documents are protected during three stages: uploading, storage and printing. Documents are used only for the printing process. After printing, they are deleted to protect user privacy. A collection code, whether scanned or typed, works for one job only and stops working once that job is collected or expires, so a code that someone else has seen cannot be used to claim a document.

Document retention and deletion

Documents are stored temporarily and deleted after printing. If a user uploads a document but does not complete the payment, the document is deleted after the allowed waiting period.

Payment security

Payments are made through a payment gateway. The kiosk does not store sensitive payment details. If payment is completed but printing fails, the job status is checked and a refund is processed if required.

Physical security

The kiosk is kept in a secure location with a lockable enclosure. Only authorised operators can access its internal parts. This helps protect the equipment and printed documents.

Access control

PrintPod uses role-based access control. Users can upload documents, make payments and print. Operators can refill paper and toner and handle basic problems. Administrators can manage settings and check reports. Each role has access only to the features needed for its work.

Audit and compliance

PrintPod maintains audit logs to record important activities and errors. Operators and administrators can check these logs to understand problems and take the necessary action. Access to these records is restricted to authorised personnel.

PrintPod allows institutions to configure pricing according to their academic requirements. Administrators can provide a limited free-print quota for students, subsidised printing for selected courses and departmental billing for faculty use.

Failed jobs and refunds

PrintPod initiates printing only after payment confirmation. If a job fails before any page is delivered, the full amount is refunded automatically. If printing stops part-way, the user receives a refund for the pages that were not printed. Duplicate payments are also refunded.

Campus-card refunds are credited within minutes of the failure being recorded. UPI refunds are returned to the original bank account, although the time taken to appear depends on the bank. If the refund does not arrive, the student can contact the campus operator with the transaction reference.

Revenue handling

Payments are processed through the configured payment gateway. The institution receives settlements according to the gateway's settlement schedule, after applicable fees and adjustments. The administrator portal records payments, refunds and net revenue to support reconciliation. Revenue reports can be exported for financial review.

Revenue projection

Assuming each kiosk prints 500 pages per day at an average revenue of ₹2 per page:

  • Daily revenue per kiosk = 500 × ₹2 = ₹1,000
  • Annual revenue per kiosk = ₹1,000 × 300 = ₹3,00,000
  • Annual revenue for two kiosks = ₹3,00,000 × 2 = ₹6,00,000

The projected annual operating cost for two kiosks is ₹1,92,000.

Therefore, the projected annual operating surplus before recovering the initial investment is:

₹6,00,000 − ₹1,92,000 = ₹4,08,000

These estimates exclude taxes, payment gateway fees, depreciation and other unlisted expenses. They also assume that the stated average revenue per page is achieved.

Break-even analysis

The initial investment for two kiosks is ₹1,50,000. Based on the assumptions above, the monthly revenue is ₹25,000 per kiosk over 25 operating days, while the monthly operating cost is ₹8,000.

The projected monthly operating surplus is ₹17,000 per kiosk. Therefore, the estimated break-even period is:

₹75,000 ÷ ₹17,000 ≈ 4.4 months per kiosk.

This is a simplified estimate assuming stable usage from launch. Actual break-even may take longer because of lower initial demand, maintenance issues and unexpected costs.

In a lower-usage scenario of 250 pages per day per kiosk, monthly revenue would be ₹12,500, assuming 25 operating days and ₹2 per page. After ₹8,000 in monthly operating expenses, ₹4,500 remains per kiosk. The initial investment would take about 17 months to recover rather than four and a half. This demonstrates that customer demand is a major factor in the project's financial viability.

Benefits that are not financial

PrintPod offers several benefits beyond revenue generation:

  • Extended availability: Students can print whenever the campus is open without depending on counter timings.
  • Reduced waiting: Self-service printing can reduce queues during assignment submissions and examination periods.
  • Improved privacy: Documents are released for collection by the person who submitted them.
  • Reduced staff workload: Staff can spend less time handling routine printing requests.
  • Better accountability: Digital transaction records help administrators investigate payment disputes and monitor usage.

Case study

Note: This is an illustrative scenario developed for an academic project. It does not describe an actual deployment.

The scenario

Consider a hypothetical university with 3,000 students and 10 academic departments. Before introducing PrintPod, students depend on a library printing counter and nearby commercial print shops. These services have limited operating hours and become crowded before assignment deadlines.

The existing arrangement also requires manual payment collection and staff involvement. Students may need to wait in queues, carry cash and worry about the privacy of their documents.

The university proposes a self-service printing system to improve convenience, reduce manual work and provide better transaction records.

The deployment

The university proposes installing two PrintPod kiosks at locations with high student traffic.

Location Justification
Central library entrance Convenient for students accessing study materials and preparing assignments
Main academic block Accessible to students from multiple departments

The proposed deployment takes place in three phases: site preparation and installation, system testing and commissioning, and a monitored public launch. During the initial period, operators will check printer status, consumable levels and payment records.

Projected results after six months

The following table presents illustrative projections rather than measured results.

Indicator Before deployment Projected after deployment
Printing availability Limited counter hours Throughout campus opening hours
Printing process Staff-assisted Self-service
Payment method Primarily manual Digital
Transaction records Manual or incomplete Centralised digital records
Waiting time Queues during peak hours Potentially shorter waits

The university could use kiosk records and student feedback to measure actual performance after deployment. Numerical improvements should be reported only after sufficient operational data has been collected.

What the scenario reveals

The scenario highlights three important observations. First, kiosk location strongly influences usage because students prefer services along their normal campus routes. Second, paper and toner must be replenished regularly to avoid interruptions during peak periods. Third, reliable network connectivity and payment confirmation are essential for smooth operation.

A limitation is that two kiosks may not meet demand during major submission periods. The university should review usage data, failed jobs and waiting times before deciding whether to expand the service.

Chapter

Future scope

[Chapter opening: 2 sentences separating what could be added soon from what would need a different design.]

Near-term enhancements

PrintPod could be improved through features such as document templates for common academic forms, multilingual screen instructions, scan-to-email functionality and uploads from cloud storage. These additions could make the kiosk easier to use and reduce the steps required to print frequently used documents.

Longer-term possibilities

Future versions could support ID card printing, document binding, lamination and integration with the institution's learning management system (LMS) or enterprise resource planning (ERP) platform. A multi-campus dashboard could also help administrators monitor several kiosks from one location.

Limitations of the current design

The proposed design has some limitations:

  • It depends on a reliable power supply and network connection for payment confirmation and remote monitoring.
  • Paper jams, toner shortages and hardware failures can interrupt printing.
  • Initial installation and recurring maintenance require financial investment.
  • Actual demand and profitability may vary across campuses.

These limitations should be evaluated during pilot testing before the system is deployed on a larger scale.
